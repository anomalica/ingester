What was tested

Two representative pages

  • A scanned, declassified government report — degraded typewriter text, classification markings, redaction boxes, and structured header fields.
  • A born-digital scientific paper — embedded equations (including a nested radical whose meaning is lost when flattened to plain text), inline references, and figures.

Source material is withheld; only the challenge types are described.

How it was scored

Each model got the same instruction — transcribe the page faithfully into structured markdown, preserving redactions, classification, figures (as descriptions), and equations (as LaTeX) — plus the page image, at temperature 0, through one API gateway. Outputs were checked against the ground-truth page on six things:

  • equation structure correct (radical scope, subscripts)
  • correct document title (not the form header)
  • redactions detected, not silently dropped
  • nothing fabricated (no invented metadata)
  • output format followed
  • whole page transcribed

The watermarking angle

Text watermarking biases a model's word choices to leave a hidden statistical signature. It only has room to act where several next-words are about equally likely, so verbatim copying of clear text is largely untouched — but for a task whose entire point is faithful reproduction, a vendor-injected signature in the output is worth knowing about. As of August 2026:

  • Google (Gemini) and Anthropic (Claude) watermark their text output, including via the API; it cannot be disabled.
  • OpenAI (GPT) does not watermark text (only images and audio).
  • Open-weight models (Qwen, GLM) carry no vendor watermark — you control the decoding.
Scores are a qualitative assessment on two pages, not a large benchmark — a signal, not a verdict; the very-cheapest tier in particular is unproven.
